ParcelRound — dossier
=====================

I. What it is

A method for splitting one body of work across several coding agents at once, without the pieces failing to meet [fact 2324].

II. What it is not

Under “Is this for you? [fact 2325]”: “Don't” for a single task, for exploratory work where the split isn't obvious yet, or where the pieces can't be tested separately [fact 2326]. Two agents on a two-way split is usually slower than doing it yourself, because the brief costs more than the work [fact 2327].

III. The contract

Under “The one failure mode [fact 2328]”: the work between the parcels belongs to nobody, and it is invisible because every parcel's own gate is green [fact 2329]. Of its own rules it says: “Every rule here is here because something broke without it, or because its practice caught a defect before it shipped, or, where the rule says so, because it rests on practice that held, on a cost, or on the owner's word [fact 2330].”

IV. Verified

No docs/VALIDATION.md ledger and no workflow. The sixth round's case study records what that round added: “a gate of its own, tools/check_method.py, and an adoption record, ADOPTION.md, one row per proposal [fact 2331].” Of what the gate reads, its own docstring says: “It reads structure and citations, never what a rule says [fact 2332].” Further on, it says which of its checks do: “Checks 7 and 9 read text, and are such limits [fact 2333].” It gives the gate's threat model: “Threat model (the owner's decision, 2026-10-02). The gate guards against two things [fact 2334]”, namely “drift by honest, fallible authors [fact 2335]” and “accidental publication [fact 2336]”; and it states what it does not guard against: “It does not guard against deliberate evasion [fact 2337].” This page quotes the gate and does not run it; Test it, below, says how you can.

What the method's records hold, and what its case studies measured:

- ADOPTION.md, which says what METHOD.md has taken in from each case study, holds 121 rows: 87 adopted, 8 adopted in part, 23 pending, 3 not adopted [fact 2338]. For a pending row it says: “A pending proposal is the owner's to settle [fact 2339].”
- The round CASE-STUDY.md measures returned “twelve corrections from five parcels — every single parcel corrected its brief [fact 2340]”, on the strength of one line: “Report anything you found that this brief got wrong [fact 2341].”
- The sixth round records the same of its own parcels: “Every parcel corrected its brief [fact 2342]”, and for its first wave “Thirteen brief errors from four parcels, each confirmed by the lead at its source [fact 2343].”
- The sixth round also planted faults in the copies its verifiers worked on: “ten of ten caught, from the sources [fact 2344].” Its own reading of that figure: “A verifier that read the design first could have found every plant by comparison alone [fact 2345].”

VI. Test it

- python tools/check_method.py: “The front door: python tools/check_method.py says whether the method still holds together [fact 2361].” Its docstring adds: “There is no cache: each run reads everything again [fact 2362].”
- python tools/check_method.py --control: “--control shows each of its checks can fail [fact 2363]”; in its docstring's words, it will “plant faults, each in a copy of the tree or in a scratch repository, at least one per check; each must be caught [fact 2364].”
- Read CASE-STUDY.md's worked example yourself and check that its account holds together — free, and README.md calls it “The clearest single argument for it [fact 2365].”
- Compare templates/brief.md and templates/verifier.md with METHOD.md: ADOPTION.md says “Each rule in a template is tagged, in parentheses, with the METHOD.md section it comes from, or "own" for the templates' own guidance, as each template's legend says [fact 2366],” so follow a tag, and read the section it names.
- Try the step the method takes before any split, on a codebase you already know: list every file each of several independent changes would touch, and see whether one file appears in several such lists at once.

VII. Prove it wrong

Cheapest first; the site-wide rules of engagement are stated once, on the Corrections page.

- Build a fault the gate's threat model covers, in a copy of the tree, and run python tools/check_method.py. Its docstring claims: “Inside the threat model, the gate must catch it or a limit below must state it [fact 2367].” A fault inside the model that passes, with no stated limit covering it, is a disproof.
- Pick a row of ADOPTION.md marked adopted, or a line of a template tagged with a section, and read the METHOD.md section it names. The gate states what it cannot see there: “it holds that an adopted row's headings exist, not that the rule is under them [fact 2368],” and “It holds that a section named exists, not that it holds the rule the line beside it claims [fact 2369].” A section that does not hold the rule is a disproof.
- Check the sources of the timeline in CASE-STUDY-6.md [fact 2370]. Its heading says “from the ledger's own timestamps, and from the commits' times where a row says so [fact 2371].” Compare a row that says so with the times of the commits it describes, and any other row with the stamp of the ledger entry it comes from. A row that disagrees with its source is a disproof.
- Recount the sixth round against the ledger its case study archives: “The ledger is archived beside this file as archive/round6-ledger.zip [fact 2372].” The figures Verified quotes from that case study are counts a recount can disagree with; a count the archived ledger does not bear out is a disproof.
- Apply the method to a real, multi-agent piece of work of your own and see whether the one failure mode still finds something the briefs missed; a clean round is evidence against the method's reach, not proof it is wrong, since “found nothing” is a stated, acceptable result here too.
