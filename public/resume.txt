== Selected projects

=== ParcelRound, HonestFramework and HonestHarness | since Sep 2026 [fact 1232]
- ParcelRound: A method for splitting one body of work across several coding agents at once, without the pieces failing to meet [fact 1216]. It exists because the obvious approach fails in a specific, repeatable way [fact 1217]: Give four agents four well-scoped parcels of one feature and you can get four green gates and a feature that does not work [fact 1218]. 6 [fact 1219] rounds are recorded as case studies, and the sixth put planted faults on every parcel's verifier [fact 1220].
- HonestFramework: A way to lay out a project so that an AI can write nearly all of it and no claim about it ever rests on the AI's judgement [fact 1221]. Whether a claim is true is answered by something that cannot hold an opinion: a reference implementation, a bit-identity comparison, a hash, a gate that has been watched to fail, an append-only record with dates on it [fact 1222].
- HonestHarness holds the requirements, H1 to H17, derived from the record of cft-fp256's rounds [fact 1223]. Its first round asked: can a cheaper model, reached over an API, do the skills a ParcelRound round measures [fact 1224]? Replaying the planted faults of ParcelRound's sixth round, DeepSeek's flash model caught 31 of 40 [fact 1225], where that round's own verifiers, on Opus, had caught 10 of 10 [fact 1226]. Every plant that changed a figure or a reference was caught in every run, 24 of 24 [fact 1227]. Plants that changed meaning or shape were caught 7 of 16 [fact 1228].
- Where a suite stops telling strong models apart, the record says so: 659 of 660 runs exact [fact 1229]. So the QS6 slice cannot tell strong models apart [fact 1230].
- This site is built the same way. In its second round each verifier, on Haiku, checked a copy of a parcel's work holding planted faults: 8 of 8 caught, each on its first pass [fact 1231].

=== cft-fp256 | since Aug 2026 [fact 1248]
- A math coprocessor that gets the same answer everywhere [fact 1234]: It does IEEE 754 arithmetic at four precisions - 32, 64, 128 and 256 bits - and guarantees that the same inputs give the same bits whether the work runs on a laptop CPU, in a browser tab, on a microcontroller, or on the FPGA card it was designed for [fact 1235], the AMD/Xilinx Alveo U50 through the open Vitis RTL kernel flow [fact 1236].
- Used cft-fp256 to test how far one person without formal computing training can take a serious engineering effort with AI. [fact 1237] The rule is that a number in a document has a run behind it, and the runs that failed stay in the record [fact 1238].
- Checked by 29 simulation targets checking every result and every flag against the golden model, and 38 machine-checked proofs, plus a negative control that must be refuted or the gate has stopped being able to catch a bug [fact 1239]; and MPFR arbitrates all four formats [fact 1240].
- The card replayed the conformance sets on silicon [fact 1241]: the 1,068,915-case set was replayed on the card, one tile, at each point of the 2026-09-23/24 clock sweep from 145 to 170 MHz [fact 1242]. A binary256 soak on the card ran 541 passes in 70 minutes, each a resident run of a million elements at fma, add and mul [fact 1243], with 0 failures [fact 1244].
- cft-rebound is REBOUND's IAS15 integrator with its arithmetic routed through libcft [fact 1245]. At binary64 it is REBOUND's own IAS15 bit for bit, which is a gate the port passes on every build [fact 1246].
> Where to look first: [the validation ledger](https://github.com/loganw234/cft-fp256/blob/19de866c3bf9519672fe847771f4784571835c30/docs/VALIDATION.md), [the conformance contract](https://github.com/loganw234/cft-fp256/blob/19de866c3bf9519672fe847771f4784571835c30/CONFORMANCE.md), and [the browser replay](https://loganw234.github.io/cft-fp256/), which replays the published conformance vectors in front of you [fact 1247].
