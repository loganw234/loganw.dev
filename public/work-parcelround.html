IWhat it is

A method for splitting one body of work across several coding agents at once, without the pieces failing to meet (ParcelRound 8767e23 README.md:3).

IIWhat it is not

Under “Is this for you? (ParcelRound 8767e23 README.md:27)”: “Don't” for a single task, for exploratory work where the split isn't obvious yet, or where the pieces can't be tested separately (ParcelRound 8767e23 README.md:33). Two agents on a two-way split is usually slower than doing it yourself, because the brief costs more than the work (ParcelRound 8767e23 README.md:34).

IIIThe contract

Under “The one failure mode (ParcelRound 8767e23 METHOD.md:23)”: the work between the parcels belongs to nobody, and it is invisible because every parcel's own gate is green (ParcelRound 8767e23 METHOD.md:26).

IVVerified

No docs/VALIDATION.md ledger and no workflow: the method has no build of its own to gate. What its case studies record instead is a measurement of the method:

  • The round CASE-STUDY.md measures returned “twelve corrections from five parcels — every single parcel corrected its brief (ParcelRound 8767e23 README.md:83)”, on the strength of one line: “Report anything you found that this brief got wrong (ParcelRound 8767e23 README.md:80).”

VITest it

  1. Read CASE-STUDY.md's worked example yourself and check its timeline is internally consistent — free, and the whole argument for the method rests on it.
  2. Compare templates/brief.md and templates/verifier.md against what the case studies say a brief and a verifier actually did.
  3. Try the method's own seam test on a codebase you already know: list every file each of several independent changes would touch, and see whether one file appears in several such lists at once.

VIIProve it wrong

Cheapest first; the site-wide rules of engagement are stated once, on the Corrections page — not yet built, so this dossier does not link it.

  1. Count CASE-STUDY.md's own corrections yourself; a count that disagrees with the figure this dossier's Verified section quotes is a disproof.
  2. Read the worked example's timestamps against the commits it cites, and check each one exists.
  3. Apply the method to a real, multi-agent piece of work of your own and see whether the one failure mode still finds something the briefs missed; a clean round is evidence against the method's reach, not proof it is wrong, since “found nothing” is a stated, acceptable result here too.
