ParcelRound, HonestFramework and HonestHarness since Sep 2026 (ParcelRound f42242e git log)

  • ParcelRound: “A method for splitting one body of work across several coding agents at once, without the pieces failing to meet (ParcelRound f42242e README.md:3)”. It exists because “the obvious approach fails in a specific, repeatable way (ParcelRound f42242e README.md:6)”: “Give four agents four well-scoped parcels of one feature and you can get four green gates and a feature that does not work (ParcelRound f42242e README.md:7)”. 6 (ParcelRound f42242e ls-tree) rounds are recorded as case studies, and the sixth put “planted faults on every parcel's verifier (ParcelRound f42242e README.md:23)”.
  • HonestFramework: “A way to lay out a project so that an AI can write nearly all of it and no claim about it ever rests on the AI's judgement (HonestFramework 65447fd README.md:3)”. Whether a claim is true “is answered by something that cannot hold an opinion: a reference implementation, a bit-identity comparison, a hash, a gate that has been watched to fail, an append-only record with dates on it (HonestFramework 65447fd README.md:8)”.
  • HonestHarness holds “the requirements, H1 to H17, derived from the record of cft-fp256's rounds (HonestHarness b69ed76 README.md:16)”. Its first round asked: “can a cheaper model, reached over an API, do the skills a ParcelRound round measures (HonestHarness b69ed76 Rounds/HonestHarness-R1/CASE-STUDY.md:27)”? Replaying the planted faults of ParcelRound's sixth round, DeepSeek's flash model caught 31 of 40 (HonestHarness b69ed76 Rounds/HonestHarness-R1/CASE-STUDY.md:220), where that round's own verifiers, on Opus, had caught 10 of 10 (HonestHarness b69ed76 Rounds/HonestHarness-R1/CASE-STUDY.md:221). “Every plant that changed a figure or a reference was caught in every run, 24 of 24 (HonestHarness b69ed76 Rounds/HonestHarness-R1/CASE-STUDY.md:222)”. “Plants that changed meaning or shape were caught 7 of 16 (HonestHarness b69ed76 Rounds/HonestHarness-R1/CASE-STUDY.md:228)”.
  • Where a suite stops telling strong models apart, the record says so: 659 of 660 runs exact (HonestHarness b69ed76 Rounds/HonestHarness-R1/CASE-STUDY.md:171). “So the QS6 slice cannot tell strong models apart (HonestHarness b69ed76 Rounds/HonestHarness-R1/CASE-STUDY.md:174)”.
  • This site is built the same way. In its second round each verifier, on Haiku, checked a copy of a parcel's work holding planted faults: “8 of 8 caught, each on its first pass (this site's docs/VALIDATION.md:2103)”.

Where to look first: ParcelRound's METHOD.md, the case study of HonestHarness's first round.

cft-fp256 since Aug 2026 (cft-fp256 19de866 git log)

  • “A math coprocessor that gets the same answer everywhere (cft-fp256 19de866 README.md:3)”: “It does IEEE 754 arithmetic at four precisions - 32, 64, 128 and 256 bits - and guarantees that the same inputs give the same bits whether the work runs on a laptop CPU, in a browser tab, on a microcontroller, or on the FPGA card it was designed for (cft-fp256 19de866 README.md:5)”, the “AMD/Xilinx Alveo U50 through the open Vitis RTL kernel flow (cft-fp256 19de866 README.md:13)”.
  • Used cft-fp256 to test how far one person without formal computing training can take a serious engineering effort with AI. (stated by Logan, 2026-10-16) “The rule is that a number in a document has a run behind it, and the runs that failed stay in the record (cft-fp256 19de866 README.md:252)”.
  • Checked by “29 simulation targets checking every result and every flag against the golden model, and 38 machine-checked proofs, plus a negative control that must be refuted or the gate has stopped being able to catch a bug (cft-fp256 19de866 README.md:197)”; and “MPFR arbitrates all four formats (cft-fp256 19de866 README.md:269)”.
  • “The card replayed the conformance sets on silicon (cft-fp256 19de866 README.md:255)”: the “1,068,915-case set was replayed on the card, one tile, at each point of the 2026-09-23/24 clock sweep from 145 to 170 MHz (cft-fp256 19de866 README.md:261)”. A binary256 soak on the card ran “541 passes in 70 minutes, each a resident run of a million elements at fma, add and mul (cft-fp256 19de866 docs/VALIDATION.md:9732)”, with “0 failures (cft-fp256 19de866 docs/VALIDATION.md:9736)”.
  • A “61-hour stress run of the card with no failure (cft-fp256 19de866 README.md:431)”; then nightly sweeps: “10 nights, 20,759,013,456,650 cases, 0 disagreements (cft-fp256 19de866 docs/VALIDATION.md:26085)”, where the “sweep's case totals add items whose tools count different things (cft-fp256 19de866 docs/VALIDATION.md:21099)”.
  • cft-rebound is “REBOUND's IAS15 integrator with its arithmetic routed through libcft (cft-rebound 039e3c3 README.md:3)”. “At binary64 it is REBOUND's own IAS15 bit for bit, which is a gate the port passes on every build (cft-rebound 039e3c3 README.md:22)”.

Where to look first: the validation ledger, the conformance contract, and the browser replay, which “replays the published conformance vectors in front of you (cft-fp256 19de866 README.md:30)”.
