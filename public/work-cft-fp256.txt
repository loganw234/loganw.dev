cft-fp256 — dossier
===================

I. What it is

A math coprocessor that gets the same answer everywhere [fact 783]. It does IEEE 754 arithmetic at four precisions - 32, 64, 128 and 256 bits - and guarantees that the same inputs give the same bits whether the work runs on a laptop CPU, in a browser tab, on a microcontroller, or on the FPGA card it was designed for [fact 784].

II. What it is not

Raw fp32 and fp64 throughput [fact 785]. Every CPU and GPU on the market serves those formats at clocks and lane counts an FPGA fabric will not match, and nothing here pretends otherwise [fact 786]. The tile earns its place at the precisions commodity hardware does not offer, and on the guarantee that the answer does not move [fact 787].

III. The contract

Named levels, quoted from CONFORMANCE.md: “Level A - IEEE 754-2019 in radix 2 [fact 788]” is the standard itself; “Level B - the cft-fp256 profile [fact 789]” is “Level A, plus every choice the standard leaves to the implementation fixed to one answer, plus operations the standard does not define [fact 790].” The relationship runs one way, in the document's own words: “An implementation conforming to the cft-fp256 profile conforms to [fact 791] IEEE 754-2019 in radix 2 for the operations the profile covers [fact 792].”

The README states the promise directly: “That guarantee is the product [fact 793].” The hardware only makes it faster [fact 794].

Fixed by the entry 2026-09-12 - two gates that could not fail, and the path that has always been the critical one [fact 799], commit f192bf0 [fact 800].
- a device predating a new capability flag ignored an unchecked reserved bit and read far past a one-element buffer [fact 801] — measured as a host-side segfault in the entry 2026-09-12 - a stride-0 operand, the MODE guard it needed, and a contract I misread [fact 802]. Fixed by commit 8b7dea1 [fact 803].

VII. Prove it wrong

Cheapest first; the site-wide rules of engagement are stated once, on the Corrections page — not yet built, so this dossier does not link it.

- Open the web page above and hand it your own vector file: “Drop a vector file on it and it scores itself [fact 809].” Anything less than a perfect score is a disproof.
- make golden locally, against the same vector sets published in the repository's vectors/SHA256SUMS [fact 810].
- Replay the identity protocol yourself in another language and compare its checksum line against the ones docs/COMPATIBILITY.md [fact 811] records; a differing hex value is a disproof.
- Rebuild host/tools/mpfr_check.c and widen its sweep past the campaign's own cases: the independent oracle is only as good as the cases put to it.
